Workhuman is a B2B SaaS platform for employee recognition. Over four years I worked across conversion design, product systems, production infrastructure, creative technology, and interactive tooling — different problems at different scales, but the same underlying move: identify the problem, build the solution.

Conversion Design

01

Careers Experience Refresh

97 of 100 visitors never explored a single job.

The Careers page was losing nearly everyone. Not because the jobs were wrong, but because the page gave no reason to stay. Information architecture was flat, the value proposition was buried, and the visual hierarchy treated every section as equally important, which is to say none of them were.

Conversion 2.7% → 64.6% homepage to jobs
Exit rate ↓59% on the new experience
Session depth 1 → 3–4 jobs viewed per session

Measured in Heap Analytics; Oct–Dec, 3 months post-launch

The structural answer was sequencing. Lead with what Workhuman actually is as a place to work, then give candidates a reason to believe it, then make it easy to find the right role. Conversion compounded: 55.3% the first month, 73.7% the second, 77.6% the third.

Layer 1: The “why” before the “what.”

Careers Homepage: Introduction

Reading order is the design: testimonial video → Why Workhuman → Values → Benefits → Teams → Jobs.

Layer 2: Browse without friction.

Job Listing: Open Positions

Filters stay optional while the tags do the scanning. Product roles are pink everywhere they appear.

Color-coded department tags for instant scanning, filters kept optional so casual browsers and targeted searchers both flow. 13.1% bounce on the browse page.

Layer 3: The next job, before they ask.

Job detail: Five-tab structure

Five tabs, five candidate questions: role, team, culture, benefits, location.

Right rail: “Candidates Also Viewed”

Three recommendations, persistent across every tab. This is where 1 job per session became 4.
